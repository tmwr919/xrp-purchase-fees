## What XRP is, in about thirty seconds

XRP is the native asset of the XRP Ledger, a network designed to move value between accounts in seconds for very little cost. It's not a share of Ripple. The company and the token are separate things, and the token trades on its own regardless of what Ripple announces.

The supply picture is straightforward: roughly 62 billion XRP in circulation against a 100 billion maximum. Market cap sits around $86 billion, which puts XRP in the top five by market cap on most trackers.

## Five things worth checking before you commit to a platform

1. **XRP depth and the pair you'll trade.** XRP/USDT is the liquid pair almost everywhere. Check the 24-hour turnover on that specific pair, not the exchange's total.
2. **What getting money in costs.** Card purchases typically run 1–5%. Bank transfers and P2P are usually near zero. On Gate's own buying page, that's the published spread between methods.
3. **Maker versus taker.** A market order takes liquidity and pays the higher rate. A limit order that sits in the book adds liquidity and pays less, or on some platforms gets a rebate. This is a bigger deal than most beginners expect.
4. **Withdrawal cost and minimums.** Every exchange sets these per coin and per network, and they move with chain conditions. No flat number exists, and any blog quoting a fixed figure is quoting something stale.
5. **KYC and limits.** Verification isn't optional if you want to withdraw or use fiat rails.

## The step most "buy xrp" guides skip: the destination tag

The XRP Ledger uses destination tags. When you send XRP to an exchange or a custodial wallet, the receiving platform often routes your deposit by tag rather than by a unique address. If the withdrawal screen shows a tag field, treat it as mandatory and copy the exact value the receiving platform gave you.

Leave it blank and the transaction still confirms on-chain — it simply doesn't get credited to you. You then have to open a support ticket with the TXID, the amount and the correct tag, and wait. Gate maintains a dedicated help page for memo and tag assets precisely because this is a common failure. If there's no tag field visible, don't invent one.

## Where money actually leaks on a small XRP purchase

- **Card fees.** 1–5% is the single biggest avoidable cost for small buyers. Bank transfer or P2P exists for exactly this reason.
- **Market orders on thin moments.** Use a limit order near the mid price when you can. You save the maker-taker gap and avoid paying the top of the book.
- **Double conversion.** Depositing an unrelated coin and converting twice costs you twice in spread. Send USDT if you already hold it.
- **Withdrawing immediately every time.** Network fees are per transfer, so batching withdrawals costs less than five small ones.
- **Ignoring the tag field.** Free to get right, expensive to get wrong.

## Practical risks, briefly

XRP is a volatile asset and crypto holdings have no deposit insurance. Exchange balances are custodial, which means you're relying on someone else's security until you withdraw. Phishing is the most common way people lose funds — verify the domain before entering credentials, and never share a 2FA code.
